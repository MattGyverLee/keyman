=== GH-8064 capability-gated probes, evaluated rather than skipped, 2026-08-27 ===

Three tests in keybd_shift.tests.cpp assert real modifiers machine-wide and so
cannot run everywhere. gtest 1.8.1 has no GTEST_SKIP(), so each detects its own
capability at runtime and falls back to a logged WARNING plus SUCCEED(). That
makes a skip indistinguishable from a pass in the tally -- which is why this
capture exists: it records that on this machine they ACTUALLY EVALUATED, with
the probe output to prove it, rather than passing vacuously.

Run after the comment-condensation pass, against the same build that gates at
x86 72 passed / 1 disabled.

  cd windows/src/engine/keyman32
  ./tests/bin/Win32/Debug/keyman32.tests.exe --gtest_filter=\
    '*FreshThreadKeyboardStateReflectsLiveModifiers*:\
     *ReconcileDoesNotRaceItsOwnInjectedRestorePress*:\
     *GenericShiftSendInputReflectsInBothAsyncKeyStates*'

No --gtest_also_run_disabled_tests: none of the three is DISABLED_. The only
DISABLED_ test left in the suite is DISABLED_ResetDoesNotPressAKeyThatIsNotHeld,
which stays that way deliberately.


--- 1. FreshThreadKeyboardStateReflectsLiveModifiers -- PASSED (304 ms) ---

  SEED PROBE  this thread : GetKeyboardState ok=1 byte=0x00, GetAsyncKeyState=0x8001
  SEED PROBE  fresh thread: GetKeyboardState ok=1 byte=0x81, GetAsyncKeyState=0x8000
  SEED PROBE  after release: GetAsyncKeyState=0x0000

Reproduces the R-3 measurement exactly. The fresh thread's GetKeyboardState
reports the key held (byte=0x81) while the process main thread's own reading
reports it up (byte=0x00) with GetAsyncKeyState disagreeing at 0x8001. The
natural reading -- that a thread which has never pumped input gets nothing
useful -- is the reverse of what happens, which is why InitThread's seed at
serialkeyeventserver.cpp:251 is a live reading rather than a no-op.

The release landed: nothing left asserted machine-wide.


--- 2. ReconcileDoesNotRaceItsOwnInjectedRestorePress -- PASSED (38074 ms) ---

  RACE PROBE  filler=  0  races=0/100  staleReads=0/100  SendInput mean=1695us
  RACE PROBE  filler= 32  races=0/100  staleReads=0/100  SendInput mean=52356us
  RACE PROBE  filler=200  races=0/100  staleReads=0/100  SendInput mean=321010us

300 trials, zero races, across three queue-depth regimes -- and the point of the
filler levels is that they change the cost by two orders of magnitude (1.7 ms to
321 ms mean) without changing the answer. SendInput does not return until the
injected press is visible to GetAsyncKeyState, so a previous batch's own restore
press cannot still be in flight when the next batch's CaptureLiveModifierState
runs, and the reconcile cannot clear a byte on the strength of a stale reading
it caused itself.

This is an oracle, not a one-off measurement: it goes red if a future Windows
makes SendInput return early. What that would break is recorded in the test's
own comment.


--- 3. GenericShiftSendInputReflectsInBothAsyncKeyStates -- PASSED (551 ms) ---

  GENERIC VK PROBE  GetAsyncKeyState VK_SHIFT=0x8000 VK_LSHIFT=0x8001 VK_RSHIFT=0x0000
  GENERIC VK PROBE  hook saw vk=0xA0 scan=0x00 extra=0x00000000 flags=0x10

**This is the one that was still recorded as "not yet run", and it settles the
open question it was written for.** A third party's SendInput with a generic
wVk=VK_SHIFT and wScan=0 IS reflected in the chiral GetAsyncKeyState(VK_LSHIFT)
-- 0x8001, held -- and the hook observes it already re-chiralised to vk=0xA0,
not 0x10. Windows resolves the side on the way in.

So CaptureLiveModifierState's deliberate decision NOT to widen its reading to the
three generic VKs (VK_SHIFT/VK_CONTROL/VK_MENU) that the hook's isModifierKey
also accepts is now measured rather than argued, on this machine. The fallback
sketched in that function's comment -- OR the generic reading into both chiral
halves when neither reports held -- is not needed here.

What this does not prove: that no future Windows, and no other injector, can
violate the assumption. The test is the guard against that, and it is in the
suite rather than DISABLED_ precisely so the assumption cannot rot unnoticed on
the machines that can check it.


--- Why this file exists at all ---

A capability skip logs "precondition unmet, not evaluated" as a WARNING and then
SUCCEED()s. Read the log, not the tally, on any leg without an interactive input
desktop -- a Session-0 CI service account will report these three as passed
without having asserted anything. On this machine, on this run, all three
asserted for real.

--- Observed the same day: a capability failure that did NOT skip ---

One x64 suite run, between the two clean ones above, reported three FAILURES
rather than three skips:

  [  FAILED  ] KEYBD_SHIFT.ReconcileDoesNotRaceItsOwnInjectedRestorePress
  [  FAILED  ] KEYBD_SHIFT.DwExtraInfoSurvivesSendInputWhereTheScanCodeDoesNot
  [  FAILED  ] KEYBD_SHIFT.GenericShiftSendInputReflectsInBothAsyncKeyStates

All three are the real-injection/hook tests. Established immediately afterwards:

  - all three PASS on x64 when re-run in isolation, with correct probe output
    (VK_SHIFT=0x8000 VK_LSHIFT=0x8001, hook saw vk=0xA0)
  - the full x64 suite then PASSED 71/71 on the very next run
  - no modifier was stuck: all nine VKs read up before and after
  - the only source change since the previous clean run was comments

So: interference, not a regression. Another process disturbing the input queue or
the low level hook round trip is enough -- and on this machine another session was
committing to this repo at the time.

**Why this is recorded rather than dismissed.** The capability probe protects
against an ABSENT capability; it cannot protect against an INTERMITTENTLY
disturbed one. A skip is a logged SUCCEED(); this is a plain red. From a CI log
alone an environmental false failure of these three is indistinguishable from a
real regression, and the triage step is the one used here: re-run the failures in
isolation before believing them.
